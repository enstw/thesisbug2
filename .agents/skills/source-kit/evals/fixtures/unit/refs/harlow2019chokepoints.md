# Review of "Chokepoints: Finance as Statecraft" by Miriam Harlow

*Journal of Invented Studies* 12(3), 2020, pp. 411–414. Reviewed by T. Okafor.

[Page 411]

Harlow's book argues that states coerce through financial intermediaries rather than through markets themselves. The argument is ambitious, and in this reviewer's judgment only partly successful.

[Page 412]

The strongest chapter is the third, where Harlow shows that correspondent banking concentrates leverage in a handful of institutions. Her claim that "substitution is always slower than sanction" is asserted rather than demonstrated.

[Page 413]

The case selection is narrow. All four cases involve dollar clearing; none tests whether the mechanism travels to other currencies.
